The lid picks the note; moving it pumps the bellows. With Voice on, the lid is also a mouth — nearly shut sings a closed vowel (oo), wide open sings an open one (ah), gliding through the vowel space between. Hold still and the air runs out in about two seconds — turn on Sustain to keep it open.

A rubber duck is a bellows with a free reed in it — the same machine as a harmonium, one note wide. So the small one powers the big one: the reeds stay silent until you squeeze, and the charge drains in about eight seconds, which means playing a phrase takes a steady squeeze in one hand and the hinge in the other. Press Teach it your duck and squeeze three times to learn its spectrum — squeakers vary a lot by size, so it matches yours rather than a guess, and remembers it. A squeak has to be loud, sudden and the right shape to count, so a voice or a hum at the same pitch won't hold it open. Wear headphones, or the mic hears the reeds and the thing starts feeding itself. Untick Locked to play without a duck.

Backing track  

The track plays in YouTube's own player, so it mixes with the reeds at the output rather than running through them — volume and transport are all we can reach. Duck under reeds drops the track as the bellows fill, so the harmonium sits on top. Set Sa above to the track's key, or the two will clash.
